\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\paragraph{Protocol} Version~21 was run with 24 keys on the same text (the opening of Isidore's \emph{Etymologiae}
XVII, 38{,}304 bits). Keys 1--12 had also been used during development to choose between variants; keys 13--24 were never
used for tuning, only for confirmation runs of earlier versions. Each book was judged by the two judges, the scorecard, the eight extra properties and the line gate, read back with
its key and with a wrong key.

\paragraph{How close to the manuscript} The first judge separates generated from real pages with AUC 0.55, the second
with 0.60 (Table~\ref{tab:results}, Figure~\ref{fig:evaluation}); on the fresh keys the second judge gives 0.606. For the
second judge this is the level of the real manuscript with its lines shuffled within each page (0.595), and far from
the manuscript with its words shuffled within each line (0.99). The group of features that the second judge uses best
is the register of the first lines of paragraphs (AUC 0.61 on that group alone over the 24 keys; on the fresh keys the
word statistics are as informative, 0.609 against 0.605). The books pass 16 of the 17
attainable scorecard properties: they always miss the page profile, a measure of how much each page departs from its
section, and once the homogeneity within the line. Of the extra properties they usually miss the spelling choices that
vary from line to line (23 of 24 keys), the register of the first lines (18 of 24) and the spread of word lengths (20 of
24). Lines are too irregular in width: 11.6\% are wider than 1.25 times the page median, against 6.0\% in the
manuscript. About 0.4--0.5\% of the word triples of a book also occur in the manuscript.

\paragraph{With and without a message} For each of the 24 keys we also wrote a book with filler bits only, with the same
parameters and key (\expcode{e419}). Against the manuscript, the books without a message score as the books with one:
the paired differences (with minus without) are $-0.009 \pm 0.008$ for the first judge and $-0.007 \pm 0.010$ for the
second, and the scorecard differs by $-0.04 \pm 0.04$; all 48 books pass the line gate. A classifier trained directly to
tell the pages of books with a message from the pages of books without one (the 228 features of the second judge, folds by
key, 9{,}420 pages) scores AUC 0.506, and no group of features exceeds 0.51; as a negative control, the same classifier
separating books of odd and even keys, all without a message, scores 0.511. These are the outcomes predicted in the
preregistration (differences within $\pm 0.010$, direct AUC between 0.45 and 0.55, no group above 0.58), although the standard errors of the
differences are larger than the 0.005 we expected. One prediction failed: the capacity of a book is not the same with and without a message, because the arithmetic decoder consumes a
number of bits that depends on the bits themselves; capacity is not observable without the key. With our instruments (228 features
per page and a logistic classifier), a book that carries a message cannot be told from one that does not. The measurement
does not exclude other statistics; the argument that none can succeed is the construction of Section~\ref{sec:generator},
and it holds only as long as the cipher is sound.

\paragraph{What the generator does not do} It writes only running paragraph text: no labels, no circular or radial
text, and no images (the website places the real drawings next to the generated folios). It does not model hands, and
it generates pages independently, so the bifolium effects of Section~\ref{sec:book} are absent. It models copying from
the line above by the index of the word in the line, whereas the manuscript copies by physical position. Two rules of
Section~\ref{sec:rules} were not targeted: the closure at the gap of a drawing, which no instrument measures, and the
drift along the line, which enters them only indirectly. The short state was targeted only as agreement of the spelling
choices within a line.
