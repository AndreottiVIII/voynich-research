\begin{abstract}
\setlength{\parskip}{0.6em}
\noindent We studied how the text of the Voynich manuscript (Beinecke MS~408) is written, in 716 logged experiments,
670 of them preregistered: hypothesis and criterion were fixed before the run, the code was tested first on synthetic
texts, and each finding was set against the same measurement in 24 natural languages, constructed languages,
volunteers' gibberish, published generators and ciphers and, where scribal habits were at issue, 79 medieval
manuscripts (six Nordic, 73 German).

\noindent The measurements quantify regularities described before (word boundaries marked redundantly by word form,
the dependency between adjacent words and its stop at the line break, line-edge forms, paragraph-initial gallows,
copying from the line above) and add some that, to our knowledge, are new: the dependency between adjacent words
mirrors the glyph sequences inside words; immediate repetition is not avoided, unlike in every language and manuscript
compared; each line avoids starting like the line above, and marked variants drift along the line, both beyond
anything in the medieval scribes measured. Eighteen of the measured properties form a scorecard.

\noindent From the scorecard we built the \emph{voynichizer}, which hides any text of up to about 20{,}000 characters in a Voynich-like
book of 207 pages.
The text is compressed, encrypted and authenticated with a key; through an arithmetic decoder its bits decide only how
many times each recurring Voynich word appears on each page, while layout, invented words and word order follow the
measured rules. On 24 keys, two classifiers retrained on each book separate its pages from the real ones with AUC 0.55
and 0.60 (0.5 is chance; the second gives 0.60 also to the real manuscript with its lines shuffled), and the books
pass 16 of the 17 attainable scorecard properties.

\noindent That a Voynich-like text can carry a real message had been shown at the level of words, most fully by the
Naibbe cipher (Greshko, 2025). We demonstrate it again, with a different mechanism and two stricter conditions: the
book also follows the line, paragraph and page rules, and a book with a message is a sample of the same distribution
as a book without one (a classifier trained to tell them apart scores AUC 0.51), while the key recovers the text
exactly. The regularities of the manuscript constrain how it was written, not whether it says something.
\end{abstract}
