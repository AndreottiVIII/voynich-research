\begin{abstract}
\setlength{\parskip}{0.6em}
\noindent We present the \emph{voynichizer}, a program that turns any text into a book of 207 folios written in the
script of the Voynich manuscript (Beinecke MS~408), in EVA transliteration. The text is compressed, encrypted and
authenticated with a key, and the resulting bits are sampled into the book through an arithmetic decoder: they decide only
how many times each recurring Voynich word appears on each page, while layout, invented words and word order follow the
manuscript's statistics. With the key the text is recovered exactly; without it, a book that carries a message and a book
that carries none are samples of the same distribution.

\noindent The generator is specified by a scorecard drawn from 716 logged experiments on how the manuscript is written
(670 preregistered), each compared with the same measurement in 24 natural languages, constructed languages, volunteers'
gibberish, published generators and ciphers, and 79 medieval manuscripts (six Nordic, 73 German). The measurements
quantify known regularities (word boundaries redundantly marked by word form, line-edge forms, paragraph-initial gallows,
copying from the line above) and add others: the dependency between adjacent words mirrors the glyph sequences inside
words and survives nulls that keep word form and line position; each line avoids starting like the line above; marked
variants drift along the line at least two and a half times as much as in any scribal choice.

\noindent On 24 keys, two classifiers retrained on each generated book separate its pages from the real ones with AUC
0.55 and 0.60 (0.5 is chance; the real manuscript with its lines shuffled scores 0.60), and the book passes 16 of the 17
attainable scorecard properties. Statistical regularities of this kind, however strong, cannot by themselves decide
whether the Voynich manuscript carries a message.
\end{abstract}
